PREVAIL

Redirecting pre-existing viral immunity toward cancer with engineered bacteria.

PREVAIL explores a universal bacterial tumor vaccine: oncolytic bacteria deliver a familiar SARS-CoV-2 antigen into tumor cells, asking the immune system to remember what it already knows and attack where tumors hide.

01

The starting point

Most tumor vaccines try to teach the immune system something new.

PREVAIL asks a different question: what if cancer therapy could reuse immune memory that many people already carry?

02

Why it matters

Tumors are not passive targets. They are protected environments.

Immunotherapy has changed cancer treatment, but many tumors remain difficult to reach. The tumor microenvironment can suppress immune activity, lack universal targets, and vary widely between patients.

Hypoxia Low pH Immune suppression Target heterogeneity

Inside a solid tumor, every layer creates another barrier.

01Oxygen fades

Deep regions become hypoxic and difficult for conventional therapies to reach.

02Acidity rises

A low-pH environment disrupts immune-cell function and favors tumor survival.

03Defenses weaken

Suppressive signals prevent immune cells from sustaining an effective response.

04Targets change

Tumor heterogeneity makes a single universal tumor antigen difficult to define.

03

The turning point

Do not build a new immune response from zero. Recall an old one inside our body.

SARS-CoV-2 infection and vaccination have generated widespread immune memory. PREVAIL uses RBD as a recognizable antigenic cue, turning a familiar viral memory into a tumor-localized immune signal.

Why SARS-CoV-2 RBD?

SARS-CoV-2 exposure and vaccination have created widespread immune memory. Rather than designing an unfamiliar tumor antigen from the ground up, PREVAIL chooses RBD as a cue that many immune systems are already prepared to recognize.

04

The carrier

Bacteria know how to enter the places immune cells struggle to reach.

Oncolytic bacteria can preferentially colonize tumor tissue, penetrate deep hypoxic regions, and be genetically engineered as low-cost delivery vehicles. In PREVAIL, they are not decoration around the therapy; they are the route into the tumor.

Tumor tropism Deep penetration Genetic modularity Immune stimulation Manufacturing accessibility

One familiar antigen.
Three biological transitions.

Act I Colonize

Engineered bacteria reach the tumor microenvironment.

Act II Release

Intracellular lysis releases the SARS-CoV-2 RBD antigen, which is presented by MHC class I molecules.

Act III Recall

Memory immune cells recognize the familiar cue and attack.

05

The mechanism

A three-act delivery story.

PREVAIL converts tumor colonization into antigen presentation. The bacteria deliver the signal; the tumor becomes the stage; pre-existing antiviral immunity becomes the attacking force.

Bacteria carry the message. Tumor cells expose it. Immune memory supplies the response.

08

Why this direction matters

A universal, low-cost, memory-guided route for tumor immunotherapy.

PREVAIL is not only a delivery system. It is a proposal for a more accessible kind of cancer immunotherapy: one that uses bacterial engineering to bring a common immune memory into a local tumor context.
