01 Background of Tumor Vaccines

Cancer immunotherapy has emerged as a promising strategy to recruit immune surveillance against malignant cells. However, conventional tumor vaccines are often limited by weak antigenicity, poor tumor infiltration, and high inter-patient heterogeneity.

SCIENTIFIC DEFINITION

Immunogenicity refers to the ability of a particular substance, such as an antigen or epitope, to provoke an immune response in the body of a human or other animal.

Conventional bacterial vectors offer promising advantages in tumor colonization because many facultative anaerobes preferentially enrich in hypoxic tumor microenvironments. This makes them attractive candidates for localized delivery of therapeutic payloads.

02 The ‘Pre-Existing Viral Immunity’ Mechanism

PREVAIL introduces a paradigm shift by hijacking the body’s existing antiviral immune memory. Instead of relying solely on tumor-specific antigens, our system presents familiar viral antigens inside the tumor site.

This mechanism leverages pre-existing immune memory generated by previous viral infection or vaccination, redirecting immune recognition toward tumor-associated bacterial colonization.

[Figure 1: Schematic Representation of Viral Memory Recall in the TME]

Mechanism Steps

  1. Colonization of the hypoxic tumor core by engineered bacteria.
  2. Regulated expression of viral-immunity surface proteins.
  3. Recruitment of pre-existing memory CD8+ T-cells to the tumor site.
◎

Benefit

Rapid activation bypasses the 7–14 day delay typically required for naïve T-cell priming.

03 Engineering the Universal Bacterial Chassis

Our system is designed as a modular, genetically stable bacterial chassis engineered for safety, controlled survival, and tumor-specific expression.

▱ Biosafety Kill-Switch

An oxygen-sensitive promoter ensures the bacteria only survive within the hypoxic conditions of the tumor.

▱ Surface Display Modularization

Multiple viral epitopes can be anchored to the outer membrane for maximum visibility.
